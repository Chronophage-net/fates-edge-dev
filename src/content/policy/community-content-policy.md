## What This Is

The Community Content Program is an open invitation: take the Fate's Edge
System Reference Document, build your own adventures and settings on top
of it, and sell them — on Itch.io, DriveThruRPG, or anywhere else — without
asking permission first.

This program doesn't replace the [core license](https://github.com/Chronophage-net/fates-edge-apps/blob/main/LICENSE.md);
it extends it. The SRD is CC BY‑NC‑SA 4.0, which by itself does not permit
commercial use. This document is the author's explicit, additional grant
of commercial rights for community creators who follow the terms below.
Nothing here changes the license on Extended Content (setting lore, named
NPCs, patrons, art, Kon'reh, etc.) — that material is still all rights
reserved and is not covered by this program.

There is no submission process, no approval queue, and no royalty. You
publish; we don't review it first. See **Enforcement**, below, for the one
exception to that.

## You May

- Use SRD material (core mechanics, dice resolution, character creation
  frameworks, equipment tables, and anything else marked `[SRD]` in the
  source) as the rules engine for your own original work.
- Write your own setting, adventures, NPCs, factions, and lore — entirely
  your own creative property.
- Publish that work commercially, on Itch.io, DriveThruRPG, or any other
  storefront or platform, and keep 100% of what it earns. We take no cut
  and ask for none.
- Publish for free, as shareware, pay‑what‑you‑want, or any other model
  you like.
- Do all of this without contacting us first, submitting anything for
  review, or waiting for approval.

  faction descriptions, proprietary mechanical systems (Runekeeper,

  Crown Spread, Travel Framework, etc.), artwork, or maps — without
  separate written permission. If your product uses any of this, it isn't
  covered by this program and needs its own license from the author.
- Use Kon'reh (the strategy game) content; it's a separate, wholly
  original property and isn't part of the SRD or this program.
- Claim or imply that your product is official, endorsed, or produced by
  Nicholas A. Gasper or Fate's Edge. It's fan/community content, and it
  needs to read that way.
- Use the Fate's Edge name or logo as part of your product's own title or
  branding. You may use it only in the compatibility statement described

- Relicense the SRD text itself under different terms. Whatever you build
  on top is yours, but the SRD portions you reuse verbatim or adapt

## What Good Looks Like

*This section is advice, not terms. Nothing here is a condition of the
program — you can ignore all of it and still be fully within your rights.*

The permission above covers what you're **allowed** to publish. It says
nothing about what makes a supplement usable, and after writing a fair
number of them, three things seem to matter more than the rest:

**Say what you changed, say why, and show your arithmetic.** A variant
that states its departures plainly can be argued with, adopted, forked,
or improved. One that hides them can only be trusted or distrusted, and
neither is much use to a table trying to decide whether to run it.

**Check the curve, not the midpoint.** If you're retuning any part of the
dice economy, test it at a two-die pool *and* an eight-die pool. Two
systems can be tuned to agree exactly in the middle and diverge into
completely different games at the edges — because one is adding and the
other is multiplying. This is the single most common way a well-meaning
conversion breaks.

**Before you invent a mechanic, check whether you're re-inventing one.**
The rules are built from four objects — a roll, a clock of 4/6/8/10
segments, a four-row outcome matrix, and a two-currency economy — used at
every scale. Crafting, travel, downtime and warfare all run on those four
without additions. If your supplement needs its own resolution mechanic,
it's worth one more look: usually the thing you want is a roll, a clock,
an outcome, or a piece of table state wearing a costume. Supplements that
invent skills, clock sizes, or difficulty ladders stop being playable
alongside published material, which is the one thing a supplement really
shouldn't do.

**Then hand it to someone who'll argue with the framing.** Showing your
arithmetic catches wrong answers. It does not catch wrong *questions* —
those get answered well, correctly, and at length, which is exactly what
makes them survive review by their own author. The People's Die shipped
with a framing that called a past position "wrong"; a reviewer pointed
out that nothing factual had been wrong, and that calling it so avoided
naming the actual error, which was answering an affordability question
when the live question was friction. That took an outside reader one
paragraph and the author several weeks. Budget for it.

**A worked example.** *The People's Die* — a complete d6 conversion,
written as a joke and playable straight — is published as the reference
model for this. It states every departure from the core rules, explains
the reasoning behind each, and closes with an appendix of exactly
computed probabilities showing what the conversion actually does to the
game (including the part where it accidentally abolishes the clean
success at seven dice). The comedy is optional. The appendix is the part
worth copying.

## Enforcement

We don't curate the program and won't review your work before or after
publication. That's the point — it costs creators nothing to get started
and it costs us nothing to let it grow. But the permission this document
grants is revocable: if a specific release misuses the Fate's Edge name,
uses Extended Content without permission, misrepresents itself as
official, or otherwise falls outside these terms, we can withdraw
permission for that release (and, for repeated or willful violations,
for that creator) by written notice. Everyone else's standing under the
program is unaffected.
