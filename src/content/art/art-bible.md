*A visual reference for illustrators, UI designers, and anyone building imagery for the Fate's Edge setting. Cross-checked against the colors, icons, and taglines already shipped in the web client's data files, so this document and the live app agree with each other.*

## 1. How to use this document

This is not a style guide for a single piece of key art — it's a reference for keeping hundreds of independent illustrations, character portraits, region maps, faction crests, and UI touches from Fate's Edge feeling like they belong to the same world. Each region entry gives you: a touchstone (real-world visual shorthand), a palette, architecture and material culture, dress/status markers, and a **taboo or "secret" visual hook** — every chapter hides a rot or a lie under its surface, and that hidden thing is usually the most useful single image for making the region feel alive rather than generic.

Three rules apply everywhere in this document, because they apply everywhere in the setting:

1. **Nothing is new.** The Utaran Empire fell three centuries ago. Every civilization in this world is either patching up what it inherited, cannibalizing it, or quietly forgetting it existed. Draw wear, repair, mismatched materials, re-purposed masonry, and old sigils painted over new ones. A pristine object, building, or army is a red flag for the viewer — it means something is wrong (a cursed relic, a wealthy villain, a place time doesn't touch correctly).
2. **Power always costs something, and it should show.** Magic is not clean. A caster who's paying for their Art should look like it — smoke that clings, ice that doesn't melt right, a hand that trembles from something other than fear. Reserve "clean," "glowing," and "heroic" magic imagery for the rare cases where the lore explicitly wants a moment of grace (Oath of Flame & Light's dawnfire, a rare successful working) — and even then, undercut it slightly.
3. **No chosen ones, no perfect kingdoms.** Compose scenes so power looks earned, contested, or borrowed, never given. Crowns sit slightly wrong. Armor is functional before it's beautiful. Every ruler in this setting is either a fraud, a puppet, a prisoner, dead-and-impersonated, or something worse. Render authority figures with that undertow in mind, even in a portrait where the secret itself isn't visible.

**A setting-wide motif to know before you draw anything:** almost every culture in the gazetteer counts to eight and fears (or ritualizes) a **ninth** — a ninth cup, knot, bell, breath, wave, wind, beat, coal, lantern-signal. The ninth is where "the Hollow" gets in: an absence, a debt, a death, a truth no one says aloud. Use groupings of eight as a background visual habit (place settings, roof-beams, banner devices, dance formations) in almost any culture below, and reserve a lone, marked, or avoided ninth object as a quiet dread-cue an illustrator can drop into an otherwise ordinary scene.

## 2. Core visual thesis

**Genre touchstones:** low fantasy, post-collapse political fantasy, closer to *Game of Thrones*-era production design or *The Witcher* than high-fantasy Tolkien-clean. Think patched Roman ruins, Byzantine rump-states, Plantagenet-era France, Tudor England, early-exploration Iberia, Scottish highlands, steppe khaganates, monsoon courts, and Silk Road oasis-towns — all filtered through three centuries of decline and stubborn survival, and all quietly haunted by a lie at the top. Treat the region-by-region entries below as a lookup table, not a mood in isolation.

**The one unifying image:** a mosaic of splintered nations built in the shell of a dead empire, every one of them held together by a story its own rulers know is false. Whenever you're unsure how to render something — a building, a banner, a piece of gear, a monarch — ask "whose empire is this a leftover of, who's holding it together now, and what do they know that their people don't?"

**Palette philosophy (setting-wide):**
- Base tones stay muted, earthy, and weathered: ochre, rust, slate, bone, verdigris, oxblood, faded indigo. Reserve saturated color for **specific markers of power or magic** — a faction's livery, a Patron's mark, a rite in progress, a caste or clan color-code — so it reads as significant rather than decorative.
- Gold appears sparingly and always means something has a price attached (coin, oath, tribute, a Patron's bargain, a bribe) — never use it as a generic "fantasy fancy" color.
- Cursed, corrupted, or metaphysically "wrong" places get a color note of their own: a sickly desaturation, a single wrong-tempered color intruding on an otherwise natural palette (black flame, red mist, a green that doesn't belong in that biome, a violet grass-patch that marks an unmourned grave).

### 2.1 Cover concept (the "hero shot")

If you only commission one piece of key art for the whole setting, make it this: **a lone traveler stands on a broken Roman-style bridge at dusk, holding a single lit lamp.** Behind them, fog curls low and slightly wrong, swallowing the road they came from. Ahead, across the gap in the bridge, a shattered marble column still bears the faded eagle of the Utaran Empire, half-reclaimed by lichen. The traveler is turned three-quarters away from the viewer, cloak worn and patched, gear functional rather than heroic — this is the Gray Wanderer archetype (or any player character), not a chosen one. Light the whole scene so the lamp is the only warm, saturated color source in an otherwise muted, cold-dusk palette — everything the light doesn't reach should read as genuinely unknown, not just dark.

The composition should feel like a **transition, not a triumph**: a journey into the unknown, a threshold being crossed, the world's fallen grandeur receding behind rather than being conquered ahead. This same "bridge at dusk, lamp in hand, empire in ruins behind" formula is a reliable fallback for any splash image, loading screen, or cover that needs to represent the setting as a whole rather than one specific region.

### 2.2 Camera language

Composition should favor **wide, desolate landscapes** that emphasize isolation and decay — a single figure or small group dwarfed by a ruin, a horizon, or a fog bank, not filling the frame. Reserve tight framing for **cost, not spectacle**: a duelist's face mid-flinch, a spellcaster's trembling hand, a cracked shield, an ink-stained thumb, a shadow that's grown too long. Avoid generic "epic" wide-angle battle shots with clashing armies — when action must be depicted, find the one small human (or elven, or dwarven) detail inside it that shows what it costs the people doing it.

## 3. Regional & cultural visual identity

Organized by the gazetteer's own seven-part structure — the Northern Reaches, the Amaranthine coast, Eastern Dolmis, Akilan, the Way of Silk, Dhahara, the Eastern Realms — plus a closing section on the Spaces Between (the roads, ruins, and liminal non-places that connect everything). Within each entry: touchstone, palette, architecture/material culture, dress and status markers, and the taboo/secret visual hook.

### 3.1 The Northern Reaches

#### Aeler — the Vault-Queen's mountains
**Touchstone:** dwarven mountain-hall functional grandeur, Byzantine-engineering precision. **Palette:** basalt black, polished iron, lamp-gold, deep-vault dark. **Architecture:** bell-towers and breath-counter shafts threading every mine level; runic ledgers set into lintels; the True Masons' guild-halls doubling as shrines. **Dress/markers:** guild heraldry (Mason, Smith, Foundry, Counter, Brewer, Teamster, Lampers) worn as badges of honor, never purely decorative metalwork. **Sound as a visual cue:** bells and counted breaths are the region's real "signage" — draw characters visibly counting under their breath in deep places. **Secret:** Aeler law holds that "the dead keep their offices as carefully as the living" — the High King Beneath the Peaks may not have drawn breath in generations, and the Crown-Lich of the Crypt still issues writs, still presses crownland seals, through a chancellor no petitioner has ever seen name a successor. Render any "Royal Writ" scene with the seal pressed by a masked or downcast chancellor, never the King himself, and let the audience's unspoken uncertainty about whether he still breathes carry the tension.

#### The Mistlands — fields under a moving sky
**Touchstone:** a bread-basket valley wrapped in a moving, half-sentient fog, administered by dwarves as an agricultural protectorate. **Palette:** desaturated pastoral greens and browns fading into a pale, directionless grey-white fog; dwarf stonework in dark basalt cutting through it. **Architecture:** hedge-laced farms with ward-chimes on every lintel; dwarf-maintained "Stone Roads" that the fog visibly avoids; salt-lines poured across thresholds at dusk. **Dress/markers:** plain, wary, practical farmwear; two names per person — a market-name displayed openly, a way-name never spoken in the mist. **Taboo:** counting to nine anywhere near open fog — the "ninth note" of a ward-chime is fatal, so chimes and bell-ropes should always read as eight-beat patterns in the background of any Mistlands scene. **Secret:** Legate Halvar, the Protectorate's governor, carries a bell that has never once been rung — by her own oath it will ring only once, at the end of her reign, and everyone under her rule knows it. Render her always with this untouched bell close at hand, dust-free and conspicuously silent, a mortality omen the whole Protectorate is quietly waiting to hear. **Creatures:** Mist Wraiths, Bell-Spirits, Salt-Wights (desiccated, salt-crusted, pale blue-lit eye sockets), Pall Guardians (tattered light-absorbing shrouds).

#### Ykrul — Wolf Standards, Winter Camps
**Touchstone:** Mongol/steppe khaganate — mounted, pragmatic, ancestor-venerating. **Palette:** wolf-grey and iron, red/black/grey clan colors, bone-white winter light, fermented-milk cream tones. **Architecture:** felt yurts in winter camps, wolf-standard totem poles marking clan boundaries, sky-burial platforms on high ground. **Dress/markers:** braided hair colored by clan — red for the Wolf Clan, black for Iron Horse, grey for the Salt Steppe; fermented mare's milk (airag) as the region's signature food/drink prop. **Ritual imagery:** Khagan selection is decided by a horse-based contest, not birthright — draw the ceremonial horse as important as the candidate; the dead go to sky burial, their bones later ground into ancestor-memory beads worn by descendants. **Apology ritual:** a hostage-string is exchanged to settle a serious wrong — a literal cord passed hand to hand, useful recurring prop. **Festival:** First Grass (spring) — first green shoots celebrated with racing and archery. **Secret:** the Sky-Speaker, who claims to interpret the ancestors' will, is a fraud — a strong beat for a Sky-Speaker character is confident public performance paired with a private, unwitnessed moment of doubt.

#### Vilikari — Laurels & Longhouses
**Touchstone:** a forced hybrid of Roman provincial law and Germanic/Norse longhouse culture — Ecktorian marble grafted onto Ykrul-adjacent timber halls. **Palette:** oak brown, tarnished laurel-bronze, cold stone grey, hearth-orange interiors. **Architecture:** longhouses built directly over the ruined forums of an older Utaran town, their roof-beams often literally resting on broken Roman columns; buildings deliberately oriented to face two skies (the old empire's east, the steppe's north) at once. **Dress/markers:** dual law made visible — an oak-leaf token for clan law, a laurel token for imperial law, carried together by anyone doing business across both systems. **Secret:** the Foedus Stone was never forged — it is a genuine, growing legal artifact that adds a finger of granite for every treaty sworn on it — but its power depends on living witnesses, and the witnesses are dying out. Every season another crack opens for a promise no one living can still verify, and the newest crack has no date at all. This is one of the setting's best "slow-motion political collapse" visual hooks: render the crack as a recurring background detail that grows across successive illustrations of the same location.

#### Black Banners — Condotta & Crowns
**Touchstone:** Italian condottieri mercenary-company culture — professional, mercenary, theatrically unheraldic. **Palette:** unadorned black and iron-grey (matches the shipped Black Banner Mercenaries faction color, `#2c3e50`), broken only by a single wax-red seal. **Architecture:** mobile camp cities — pavilion command tents, portable forges, gallows-wagons; no fixed capital. **Dress/markers:** deliberately plain kit that reads as "trades in professionalism, not heraldry"; the one exception is the wax seal of command. **Ritual imagery:** the "Bell Dawn" truce convention — a bell rung at dawn that both sides of any Black Banner contract are bound to honor, a strong visual beat for battlefield scenes (weapons lowered, a single bell visible). **Secret:** "the Black Colonel" is a legend, not a living commander — no company has laid eyes on him in months, yet his banner still rides into fights that were never his to call. Render that banner without a shadow and keep the Colonel's face perpetually turned away or out of frame; the companies rallying to it are rallying to nobody, and none of them will say so out loud.

#### The Linns — Skerries & Storm-Oaths
**Touchstone:** Norse-coded sea raiders and skerry-dwellers. **Palette:** slate-grey sea, weathered driftwood, seal-fur brown, storm-green. **Architecture:** longhouses on wave-cut skerries, dragon-figurehead prows, drying-racks of fish and kelp. **Dress/markers:** nine sea-knots tied into rigging and belts as a personal ledger of vows — the ninth knot is reserved for funerals and sea-sacrifice, and should be visually distinct (a different color cord, tied looser) from the other eight. **Secret:** the Sea-Queen's authority is real and personally held — but she rules with one eye, and refuses to say whether she lost it to a storm or to a spy's blade. Render her always with the scar or patch turned toward the viewer, unhidden, as if daring anyone in the room to finally ask.

### 3.2 The Amaranthine Coast

#### Kahfagia — the Flag of Storms
**Touchstone:** early-exploration-age Iberia if a Gallic-Carthaginian sea power had never fallen — an oligarchic naval empire. **Palette:** indigo officer coats with brass, salt-bleached canvas, tarred black, storm-grey. **Architecture:** the Titan's Throat's lighthouse chains and boom gates; black-basalt Stormspire citadel; Kassamira's dockyard labyrinth. **Dress/markers:** salt-bleached canvas and tarred boots for sailors, indigo-and-brass for officers, storm-charms worn on cord by everyone; ship identification runs on a coded lantern-signal system — worth inventing a consistent lantern-color code if illustrating fleet scenes. **Death custom:** the drowned are given paper-boat candle funerals, set alight and released on the tide — a strong, gentle-horror image against the region's harder militarism. **Secret:** the Admiralty's own Mirror-Keepers are quietly being replaced by Lethai-ar sleeper agents who don't even know they've been woven — the tell is a Mirror-Keeper whose beacons never fail and whose reflection never quite settles into one face. Render mirror-towers with a faint doubled reflection, and treat the unlit ninth lantern not as an official signal but as the Web's own silent mark that it, not the Admiralty, owns that lane tonight. **Dual devotion:** split any temple or shrine visually down the middle between Ráeyn (storms, order, pale blue-white) and Khemesh (krakens, depths, black-green).

#### Ecktoria — Marble & Fire
**Touchstone:** Byzantine successor-state wearing the clothes of a fallen Rome. **Palette:** faded imperial purple, tarnished bronze, sun-bleached marble, ash grey, and the Everflame's own hot orange-white at any shrine or courthouse. **Architecture:** quake-shattered forums, colonnades still standing next to rubble, repurposed temple stone in newer walls. **Dress/markers:** togas and stolae gone threadbare and re-hemmed, clerks and magistrates in ink-stained formal wear, soldiers in old legion-pattern armor kept alive by provincial smiths who no longer remember the original alloy. **Trade custom:** three-ribbon coin-house deal codes (white/red/black ribbons tied to a contract) signal a deal's true nature to those who know to look; two-ledger tax evasion is treated as a popular "sport," not a crime. **Symbols:** the Everflame's triple-flame sigil, the Dux tithe-ladder motif (a stepped pyramid), old eagle standards with feathers missing. **Secret:** the Everflame is a literal, physical lie-detector — it blackens the tongue of anyone who lies before it — and the Grand Magistrate himself hasn't tested it in a year. His gavel is built as a sundial that only falls at true noon, and he has not struck it once this year, letting cases stall rather than risk speaking before the flame; render the gavel gathering dust in permanent shade, and let the docket behind him grow visibly thicker across successive scenes.

#### Acasia — the Broken Marches
**Touchstone:** a rotted-out imperial province run by warlords, river-kings, and toll dukes; its one real city (Silkstrand, see §3.7) is a hard, cosmopolitan free port. **Palette:** green lowland + red ochre uplands + reed-brown wetlands, all slightly desaturated by the Curse. **Architecture:** causeway-and-cart-track roads over broken Roman-style stone; toll bridges and gatehouses everywhere. **Dress/markers:** Rothari clan colors that deliberately shift by season (heraldry as camouflage — never paint them consistently). **Curse imagery:** the villagers let the Blackwood die through collective inaction generations ago, and the land has never forgiven it — the single most useful visual tell is a character's *shadow*, which the Curse takes first: render Curse-touched figures with a shadow that lags, fades, or points the wrong way before anything else about them looks wrong. **Secret:** the Lame King's entire claim to sovereignty rests on a seal that isn't a seal at all — his illiterate grandfather, a free-company captain, pressed his boot into wax because he couldn't write his name, and every "royal" writ since has copied that same bootprint. Render the Lame King's seal in close-up whenever his authority is invoked, and let a careful eye notice the tread is unmistakably a boot heel, not a sigil. **Symbols:** the mill-wheel (water rights), lamp-coins left at bridges, guest-bread at oven doors. **Creatures:** Blackwood Wolves, Curse Echoes, Thorn Beasts, Bone-Singers.

#### Vhasia — the Fractured Sun
**Touchstone:** Plantagenet-era France politically, Sun-King vibe aesthetically, two rival crowns circling a broken throne. **Palette:** deep wine reds, dynastic blues and golds; the Northern Seigneurie in black-and-gold, the Southern Parlement in red-and-silver — always keep these two courts' color schemes visually opposed in any composition that includes both. **Architecture:** faded-grandeur museums, archives, and hero-statues occupied (not annexed) by Viterran stewardship banners hung awkwardly next to old Vhasian ones; fast, loud market cities; frontier hillfort-and-law-court fusions. **Dress/markers:** "the Blood" nobility in lineage-coded colors and heavy formalwear designed for feasts-as-negotiations; duel codes favor light, precise blades and minimal armor (first blood, not slaughter). **Secret:** "the Last Dauphin" is a real, specific child of nine, hidden in the Queen-Mother's own keep — not a fabrication, but no one, not even Elianor herself, can prove he isn't a baker's son swapped in as a card to play. Render him as a genuine boy (poison-tasting glass in hand, a third language slipping into his speech) rather than a blank cipher, but keep the one detail armies actually march for — his birthmark — deliberately obscured or unshown in every portrait.

#### Viterra — the Hedge-Law Realm
**Touchstone:** Tudor England transplanted to a continent, straddling highlands and fens, ruled by a foreign dynasty it never fully accepted, its whole legal culture organized around literal hedge-boundaries. **Palette:** oiled cloak brown, fen-green, slate grey, dull iron. **Architecture:** hillforts and watch beacons in the Highlands; causeways and dike-guild villages in the Fens; ceremonial law-courts; hedgerows themselves function as load-bearing legal infrastructure — draw property lines as thick, ancient, deliberately-maintained hedges rather than fences or walls. **Dress/markers:** seal rings denote contract rights and are checked constantly in disputes — a good recurring close-up prop; oiled cloaks, fen boots, plain badges of office; Knights of the Dawn in workmanlike harness, not tourney show. **Secret:** Queen Veralyn Everblood's reputation is not manufactured — she's a genuine veteran of the sack of Lence — but her legitimacy is: she took the throne only after her brother King Baldwyn Brightstide died of a sudden, suspicious illness two years into his reign, and the unproven whisper that Fenwood (her own former guardians) cleared her path to the crown has never fully gone away. Render her public appearances with real command presence, not stagecraft, but let a watching courtier's face carry the doubt the Queen herself can't voice. **Ninth-taboo variant, worth using directly:** Viterran courts void any legal argument on its ninth citation of precedent — those who push to a ninth are said to hear laughter from the marble walls, which locals half-joke, half-mean is Baldwyn's own ghost still judging from the shadow of the throne; a courtroom illustration can play this completely straight, no visible ghost required. **Symbols:** dawn/sunrise imagery (Knights of the Dawn); competing altar iconography between the plain, itinerant Temple of Light and the wealthier, processional Church of the Everflame.

#### Thepyrgos — City of a Thousand Stairs
**Touchstone:** Greece-inspired university-state, last light of a drowned civilization (Astroegro). **Palette:** sun-bleached marble, olive and saffron, sea-blue, muted stone-hues for the resident high elves. **Architecture:** terraced hillside city stitched with stairs and aqueducts; slender watch-towers ("pyrgoi"); vine-draped stoae doubling as classrooms; a catacomb-library where deceased scholars' skeletons are posed and labeled as part of the archive — an unusually specific and striking image, worth using directly. **Dress/markers:** scholar's mantles pinned with college seals; the city runs on a bell-code rhythm (different bell patterns for market hours, curfew, Synod business) — good ambient background detail. **Magic culture:** the Synod licenses *all* magic practiced in the city; unlicensed summoning is punished by binding the summoned entity as a permanent "structural consultant" to the city itself — a fantastic, very on-theme visual gag (a captive spirit literally holding up an aqueduct or advising on drainage). **Secret:** the Archon wears a key around his neck that he never explains — it isn't ceremonial, it opens the sealed Ninth Vault holding a still-ticking Astroegro orrery, and both the Ninth's Coterie and the Witness's Archivists are watching to see if he ever uses it. Render Archon Konstantinos always with this key visible, worn like a wound rather than a badge of office. **Mood note:** magic here is enchantment, not spectacle — never draw a Thepyrgan mage throwing a fireball; draw them stabilizing, preserving, restoring, or (per the above) begrudgingly doing infrastructure consulting.

### 3.3 Eastern Dolmis

#### Theona — Three Greens, No Ninth
**Touchstone:** Ireland-coded triple-isle yeomanry obsessed with boundary law and a very specific counting taboo, ruled by a king whose own authority to bind the isles is genuinely in doubt. **Palette:** peat black-brown, pale Theonan marble white, sea-mist grey-green. **Architecture:** hedged fields with boundary stones everywhere (draw them constantly, in the background of every scene); tide mills; quarry ports under perpetual white dust. **Taboo (foreground this in every Theonan scene):** counting stops at eight; an eighth item is followed directly by "many," never nine. A person's shadow growing to an unnatural length marks them as taken by the Green Host — treat an over-long shadow as this culture's single most dangerous visual omen. **Secret:** there is no changeling king — King Conall is exactly what he appears: an elected Three-Isles King who lost his hand to a Linn raid and rules with a prosthetic carved from hawthorn. Render that hawthorn hand as his defining visual attribute; he signs Viterran writs with it, and the signature is legally binding even though his own authority to bind the isles at all is the thing everyone quietly doubts. A gray thread knotted permanently at his wrist marks a decision he's been putting off for a year. **Symbols:** fence-lines and boundary stones as recurring background texture; design crowds, tables, and formations in eights or tens, never nines.

#### Zakov — Salt & Serpent
**Touchstone:** a tide-ruled pirate/guild island economy — Barbary-coast energy with a cursed crown. **Palette:** brine-white salt flats, tar-black hulls, serpent-green coral, guild-banner clash of color (the Seven Guilds deliberately never agree on a shared palette). **Architecture:** the Sunken Quarter, which floods at every high tide and is built to be abandoned twice daily; guild-hall docks stacked with contraband. **Dress/markers:** blood-and-coin oath marks tattooed or branded as binding contracts; guild banners flown from every mast, rooftop, and doorpost, ritualized lawlessness rendered as its own kind of order. **Secret:** the Salt Prince's crown was never bound to a ring — it was bound, generations ago, to a bargain the first Prince struck with the crystalline entity beneath the harbor. Every predecessor who lost that bargain now exists as a crystalline finger-bone circulating in the city's "salt coins" (spend one and you may be carrying a captured soul, not currency — give spent silver a faint, too-warm glow). Current Salt Prince Lev's authority is failing because the Syndicates already suspect what he won't admit: he is no longer the thing's master. Draw him with the specific unease of a ruler who can feel command slipping away.

#### Aelinnel of Aevrossa — Stone, Bough, and Bright Things
**Touchstone:** a gnomish counting-and-craft culture organized around an eights-based numerology and a strict metal hierarchy. **Palette:** slate and umber stonework, moss-green forest holdings, bright accent gems reserved for master craftwork only. **Material culture:** copper is the everyday, respectable metal; iron marks a barbarian outsider, silver marks a priest, and gold marks either a fool or a bribe — use this hierarchy to instantly signal a character's status or an object's intent (a gold-heavy gift should read as suspicious, not impressive). **Secret:** the Stone Prince (Eilidh Stone-Prince) is a single mortal ruler, not a committee — but she has not spoken one declarative sentence in seven years, answering only in careful conditionals and questions, because a declarative claim is a promise the fae can bind her to. Render any "audience with the Prince" scene showing her mid-sentence, trailing into a conditional clause while an attendant waits tensely for her to finish; her unbroken copper seal — usable once, to bind any agreement — should read as the room's most dangerous object.

#### Aelaerem — Hearth & Hollow
**Touchstone:** pastoral hearth-culture with an ever-present, never-discussed negotiation running underneath it. **Palette:** warm hearth-orange and wheat-gold undercut by a cold, muted red thread-color used only for warding. **Material culture:** red-thread hearth-knots tied at every doorway; a household's wealth and safety are measured in how well-tended its knots are. **Ritual imagery:** "the ninth cup" is poured not for a guest but as an offering — a literal negotiation with something called only "the Hollow." **Secret:** the region's folkloric authority, "the Pale Shepherd," is not a person at all but a title belonging to the Hollow itself — any depiction of the Pale Shepherd should avoid a coherent face or fixed form, and should never be shown accepting anything with a visible hand.

#### The Valewood — Empire Under Leaves
**Touchstone:** a vast, ancient forest empire organized around eight sacred, individually named groves — and a ninth, deliberately unnamed one. **Palette:** deep canopy green, dappled gold light, bark-brown, and a cold absence of color in the unnamed Hollow grove (render it slightly desaturated compared to the rest of the forest). **Material culture:** way-cords marked with knot-counts function as both a trail system and a personal ledger of debts and promises; minimal metal per wood-elf custom (see §4). **Secret:** the Hazel Queen (Amalthea, "Mab") and Alder King (Thorn, "Grimmir") are not ghosts — they are the two fae sovereigns who were present a thousand years ago when the ancient genesis-vats beneath the Amber Ziggurat were sealed, and both know exactly what lies behind that still-sealed ninth door, and neither will say. Render both with real, ancient bearing rather than a hidden fraud, but let a shared refusal to look toward the ziggurat be their tell. The forest's actual ghost is someone else: the Genesis Keeper, an archivist spirit who sealed the vats and has spent a thousand years finishing the equation to re-seal them — render her as a shimmer of heat tracing numbers in the air, never a solid form. **Creatures:** Redcaps, Echo-Legionaries (translucent soldiers replaying old battles), Fox-Headed Couriers, Ancient Tree-Spirits, Web Weavers, Bark Golems, Seasonal Sprites.

### 3.4 Akilan

#### Oshiira — the Ledger Empire
**Touchstone:** the ur-nation-state; logistics and martial schools over spectacle, run by a bureaucracy so total it has outlived rulers before and may again. **Palette:** red-tile roofs, canal-front colonnade stone, spare grey-brown, silk accents reserved only for vows/funerals; ink-black and blood-red as personal status markers. **Architecture:** numbered canal reaches, caravanserai way-stations, the Granary Crescent and its stone spine of a tower, casting a shadow over every table in the confederacy. **Dress/markers:** ink-stained fingers denote status — black ink means many contracts, red ink means a death warrant or blood debt; martial adepts in guardless blades with silk scarves knotted to signal lineage and vows (a real visual language worth inventing a consistent knot-code for). **Secret:** the Widow is alive, but old — decades of holding the confederacy together through marriage alliances and personally-authorized water-rights grants are catching up to her, and her memory is failing faster than anyone outside her innermost household will admit. She has named no heir. The Great Tally's ninth page — the one page that once recorded a named successor — was lost to weevils during the Famine of the Ninth Year, and the Vermilion Corps has spent nine years searching for it without saying why. Render the Widow with real, aging authority (a hand that still commands a room, a mind that occasionally loses the thread mid-sentence) rather than as a hidden fraud — the danger here is a confederacy quietly arming for the morning she doesn't wake, not a body double. **Symbols:** the guardless blade in a scarf-wrapped sheath, the First Measure stamped cup/rod, a black-wax-sealed ledger page no clerk will read aloud.

#### Ashaan — the Veiled Throne
**Touchstone:** a fallen slaver-empire reborn under three masked, unknowable powers. **Palette:** decadent gold and black, river-island gleam over catacomb dark. **Architecture:** golden-domed river-island capital; the Temple of the Golden Sepulcher (gem-eyed skull relics); Galanina's tomb-street palimpsest; Khasurah, the "swallowed" desert metropolis, dune-buried but still lit at night. **Veil-color code:** red veils mark mourning, white veils mark marriage, black veils mark the Sisters' own clergy, and grey veils mark the "Breath-less" — a rebel underclass who have rejected the bound-spirit economy. **Material culture:** spirits are bound into brass cages and lanterns as a normalized part of daily infrastructure (lighting, labor, punishment) — treat a brass spirit-lantern as an everyday household object, unremarkable to locals, deeply unsettling to outsiders. **The Maeri (three masked sisters) — draw distinctly and consistently:**
  - *Veiled Sister* (Coins & Winter): cream-white skin, black-and-gold hair, veil made of coins from dead empires, a chill in the air around her.
  - *Helmed Sister* (Steel & Sickness): golden skin, black hair, smooth dark-bronze helm and scale armor, a nauseating presence.
  - *Masked Sister* (Red & Black): black skin, golden hair, loose red-and-black gauze mask, an atmosphere of unguarded appetite.

  All three are always exactly seven feet tall with a fully hidden face — never show one unmasked.

**Secret:** no one — not even the Esoti collegium that serves them — actually knows what the Maeri are; demigods, archfey, lich-queens, and the last masks of the First Fey are all live theories, and the masks and veils should stay utterly opaque, never hinting at what's underneath. The deeper unease to render is the Veil itself: the magical membrane separating living and dead is failing for reasons no one can explain, so even an ordinary brass spirit-lantern in a mundane scene should occasionally flicker as if its captive is about to get free.

#### Ngomebe — the Stone Men
**Touchstone:** cyclopean-masonry mountain culture that periodically dismantles and relocates entire districts, secretly fleeing something to the south. **Palette:** undyed wool, slate-grey hems, copper pin accents, iron-black tools. **Architecture:** high stone walls built from massive, individually numbered blocks (always render the numbering); a "walking" hill-city mid-relocation, half-dismantled walls and numbered stones staged for a new site, is a striking, specific image. **Sound as material culture:** the city moves to an eight-beat-to-the-mile work-song, with a secret, unsung ninth beat that only the eldest work-crews know. **Death custom:** the dead are mixed into the mortar as the city's literal "anchor" — a haunting detail for any construction or repair scene. **Secret:** the walking cities aren't relocating for resources — they're fleeing the Cracking Wind, said to be the ghost of the mythic First City, ground to dust by its own stubbornness after it refused to ever stop. Elders believe a city that stops for nine generations will be reclaimed by the Wind. Give the Wind a visual signature (dust that erodes stone unnaturally fast, keystone edges sanded soft) rather than ordinary weather, and any wall or watchtower facing the direction the Wind is said to travel from should read noticeably more defensive than the rest. **Symbols:** the numbered stone, the white gate-cord, the wrist hammer-knot.

#### Taharka — the Monsoon Crown
**Touchstone:** highland hydraulic-engineering empire — terraces, canals, assay halls, and a mountain range that is treated as sentient and vengeful. **Palette:** indigo wraps, copper, terrace-green, mountain stone grey. **Architecture:** Siatwe's stepped-terrace capital on Lake Lilalosa; the spiral Terrace Steps Market; the Meltwater Court's brass gauge displays. **Dress/markers:** indigo wraps, copper pins, terrace-patterned brocades. **Ritual imagery:** eight named winds are honored by name; a ninth, unnamed "mountain's breath" wind is a death omen and should never be depicted as a normal gust — give it a visual signature (dust that moves against the grain, banners that flutter the wrong direction). **Custom:** the coffee ceremony runs three cups by name — Abol (welcome), Tona (a test of honor), and Baraka (a bond) — and refusing the second cup, Tona, is a calculated, serious insult to the house and its listening ancestors; a strong small-scale dramatic beat for negotiation scenes. **Symbols:** the white cord (engineers at work), the brass gauge, coin-kiss at the Mint threshold.

#### Sekogo — the Green Council
**Touchstone:** druidic river-and-lagoon trade hinge, ecologically strict, its highest authority a captive rather than a ruler. **Palette:** mangrove green, tidal brown, shell-white, indigo dye. **Architecture:** stilt hamlets, raised causeways, barrier-island lagoon harbors marked by painted mangrove shrine-poles; the Ukwe speaking-tree at the council's heart. **Dress/markers:** masks denote lineage identity and are worn as a public, legible marker rather than a disguise; nganga power-figures (nkisi) studded with debt-nails, one driven in per outstanding obligation — an excellent prop for tracking a character's debts visually over the course of a story. **Secret:** the ruling "Ukwe" is not a wise speaking-tree spirit but a prison, containing a 900-year-bound spirit — render the tree with an unsettling stillness or a too-patient quality when it "speaks." **Symbols:** the green cord (Unkwa ruling duty), the reed ring (a binding deal token — cutting the knot voids the bargain), the painted shrine buoy.

#### Lethai-ar of the Crimson Valley — Silk Bridges and Web-Law
**Touchstone:** a spider-silk engineering culture built atop something ancient and wounded. **Palette:** deep red (the valley's namesake), spider-silk silver-white, canopy green. **Material culture:** public "Marks" — skin tattoos that function as a visible, permanent promise-ledger; silk bridges woven from spider-sap connecting canopy settlements, both beautiful and load-bearing. **Secret:** the valley's crimson color is geological, not supernatural — rust-red stone and river silt, no sleeping wounded serpent beneath it. The real charged mystery is the web-law itself: an unwritten network of witnessed promises tended (not created) by the spider-spirit Inaea, made visible only through the Lethai-ar's skin Marks. For a single striking figure, draw the Unmarked — an outcast with blank, unmarked skin, invisible to the web-law and the valley's spirits, unsettling precisely because everyone else's skin visibly narrates their promises and hers says nothing.

### 3.5 The Way of Silk

#### Fhara — the Oasis Courts
**Touchstone:** a Silk Road oasis-court culture where water, not gold, is the ultimate currency. **Palette:** sand-gold, oasis-green, deep well-shadow blue-black. **Ritual imagery:** the three-cup negotiation ritual — cups of water offered and accepted in sequence — should be treated with the same visual weight as a duel or a court hearing. **Material culture:** well-spirits act as sentient judges over water disputes; the wells themselves were bought from an ancient sand-bargain whose price was memory. **Secret:** anyone who drinks too deep from certain wells begins to forget — a subtle way to hint at this is a character who repeats a story slightly differently each telling.

#### Sidhi — the Diaspora of the Sealed Coast
**Touchstone:** a freed-slave diaspora culture that has turned its own chains into its highest symbol of dignity. **Palette:** sea-green lighthouse flame, forged-brass warm gold, coastal fog grey. **Material culture:** brass seals worn as identity and status markers, literally forged from melted slave chains — a powerful, specific image (a smith remaking chain-metal into a seal is worth illustrating directly). **Ritual imagery:** a green-flame lighthouse ritual honors ancestors at night — render Sidhi coastal settlements with this eerie green light as a recognizable regional signature. **Secret:** every Sidhi lighthouse counts its own flashes, and the ninth is always kept dark — reserved, by custom, for "the collector's arrival"; the true danger behind that superstition is that the Veiled Sisters of Ashaan need nine forbidden names to unseal the Hollow and have already gathered eight. A strong visual tell: a lighthouse beam with a beat conspicuously missing, and a High Keeper's ledger of living children's names guarded like scripture.

#### Pereshi — the Roof of the Way
**Touchstone:** a fire-temple, oath-witness culture perched at a high mountain crossing. **Palette:** temple firelight orange, high-altitude stone grey, a single buried ember-red kept hidden beneath the temple floor. **Ritual imagery:** oaths are sworn before temple fire as a formal witness, similar in social weight to Ecktoria's Everflame but warmer and more communal in tone. **Secret:** a "Forgotten Prince" from Ashaan lies sealed under nine locks beneath the temple; his heart survives as "the ninth coal," and — most unsettling of all — every ninth coal in every fire-shrine across the highlands has recently begun warming in unison, pulsing to a shared rhythm that is not a heartbeat. This is one of the setting's most disturbing images and should be used sparingly, reserved for high-mystery-tier reveals, but the temple's architecture can still visually hint at it: one hearth, always covered, that no one tends openly, its glow just slightly too warm to be ordinary embers.

#### Kuvani — the Remount Steppe
**Touchstone:** a steppe horse-culture that treats horses as literal kin and ancestors. **Palette:** open-sky blue, dust-gold grassland, bone-white sky-burial platforms. **Ritual imagery:** sky burial here is explicitly about reuniting with an ancestral herd said to run in the northern lights — a strong opportunity for aurora imagery tied directly to death and lineage rather than generic fantasy wonder. **Secret:** the living Khagan is largely a figurehead — real power rests with the Remount Masters who control the stations and the Wind-Singers who read Kuva's will — while a legendary "Shadow Khagan," a cursed king named Malachai who sold his clan's horses for immortality, is said to still ride the steppe alone on horses that cast no shadow. Because of this unsettled question of who truly rules, any rider seen carrying the true Khagan's tack — his saddle, his whistle, a compact sealed with his daughter's hair — is instantly read by every witness as staking a claim to the throne itself; render the reactions of alarm and scrutiny around such an object, not just the object.

#### Tulkani — the Itinerant Courts
**Touchstone:** a nomadic wagon-circle diaspora, eternally wandering pilgrims whose homeland was swallowed by desert. **Palette:** sun-bleached wagon-wood, patchwork dyed fabrics, dust-gold. **Material culture:** knot-cord personal ledgers record debts and lineage exactly as the Ykrul's hostage-strings and Aelaerem's hearth-knots do elsewhere — a good opportunity to visually rhyme these three unrelated cultures' cord-based recordkeeping without making them look identical. **Belief:** the Tulkani are the primary vector spreading Ikasha's cult across the continent, via "dream-cords" traded quietly at caravan stops. **Secret:** their original homeland was swallowed by the desert — render any Tulkani elder's wagon-shrine with a small, carefully preserved relic or scrap of "home" that has clearly outlasted any living memory of the place itself.

### 3.6 Dhahara

#### Dhahara / The Brass Coast — Shore of Silver and Spice
**Touchstone:** a peninsula hinge between overland Silk Road caravans and monsoon sea lanes, oath-law culture, contested three ways between Kahfagia, Sidhi, and native raja courts. **Palette:** layered cotton in warm desert tones, silver seal accents, incense-terrace ochre, lighthouse-flame orange. **Architecture:** Sarvash's crossing-of-three-roads caravan capital; Hamun Gate's grim census-and-gallows fortress; Thalara's thousand-cistern incense-terrace steps; the taboo jade-paved Khetra Basin precinct. **Dress/markers:** Dhahari layered cottons with a silver seal at the throat; Himdal Aeler officers in blunt frontier dress; Kahfagian coastal presidials in cosmopolitan dock-clerk fashion. **Symbols:** oaths sworn blade-bared, palm-up, under open sky. **Secret (reserve for dark reveals):** the coast's green lighthouse flames don't just guide ships — they show the keeper the faces of the drowned, and the keeper knows every name. Admiral Voss's own stolen glass eye, looted from a pirate years ago, is rumored to still "watch" for him; it weeps salt water while he sleeps. Keep ordinary lighthouse imagery warm and reassuring at a glance; reserve the green flame's visions and Voss's weeping eye as a gut-punch reveal, not a background detail repeated casually.

#### The Nine Kingdoms — the Peacock Throne and Its Rivals
**Touchstone:** a Mughal/Rajput-inspired collection of rival courts, unified only by a founding lie. **Palette:** peacock blue-green, court crimson, caste-marked white and ochre, gold reserved strictly for the throne itself. **Material culture:** caste-marks worn visibly as a matter of course, not a secret. **Secret:** the "nine kingdoms" framing is itself a ritual fiction hiding a permanently missing tenth kingdom's unpaid debt; the current Maharaja has been dead seven years and is impersonated by nine ministers, including a secretly powerful eunuch who holds real authority behind the throne — court scenes should overpopulate the dais with ministers relative to how a "normal" royal court would be staged, without ever drawing attention to why.

#### The Himadri Foothills — the Iron Passes
**Touchstone:** high-altitude mining and pass-guarding culture, an Aeler-focused chapter parallel to the Northern Reaches' Aeler entry but distinctly Himalayan-coded. **Palette:** iron-grey stone, thin cold-blue high-altitude light, warm lamp-orange in the mine shafts. **Safety culture:** breath-counting is a literal survival tool in the mines — nine counted breaths without fresh air means death, so miners are shown constantly, visibly counting. **Secret:** the demon sealed in the Ninth Seal Mine is not legend but fact — a fragment of Varash the Unmaker, bound there centuries ago. The seal is cracking: it glows faintly at night, warm to the touch when it shouldn't be, and whispers through the stone in a language that sounds unsettlingly like butter melting. The order of Vent-Priors exists solely to keep it sealed, one patient watch-shift at a time; if depicted at all, show the seal itself — warm, glowing, faintly audible — rather than the thing behind it.

### 3.7 The Eastern Realms

#### Sihai — the Central Kingdom
**Touchstone:** an ancient, vast, rigidly hierarchical empire — the setting's imperial China analogue, its Mandate of Heaven treated in-world as a literal loan from the ancestors that must be serviced. **Palette:** jade green, lacquer red and black, silk brocade gold, ink-wash grey. **Architecture:** curved roofs warding evil spirits, minimalist cosmic-order gardens, the Sihon river-basin heartland. **Dress/markers:** chop-seals (personal stamps) made of jade, brass, or wood mark exact social class at a glance — jade for the highest, wood for commoners — a fast, legible visual shorthand worth using in any Sihai crowd scene. **Secret:** every document the Censorate stamps leaves a faint watermark that resembles a human face — no official will say whose. Meanwhile the current Emperor's own tomb sits perpetually unfinished, its crack in the stone said to whisper the growing list of ancestors who died without a named heir. Imperial court scenes can hint at the unease with a "Canal Mother's" ceremonial bench, its ninth nameplate conspicuously, permanently blank. **Symbols:** the Mandate never depicted without a subtle reminder of its conditionality (a cracked tile, an omen bird, a distant storm).

#### Nihori — the Isles of the Dawn Spirit
**Touchstone:** a feudal, volcanic archipelago in Sihai's cultural shadow — samurai, Shinto-style animism, Sengoku-era warfare, a drowned-empire storm-spirit culture layered underneath. **Palette:** volcanic black rock, moss green, folded-steel grey, lacquer red and black. **Architecture:** moss-covered temples against jagged peaks; fortified daimyō compounds. **Dress/markers:** samurai in lamellar armor with clan mon; ashigaru in mass-produced spear-and-firearm kit; shinobi in unmarked, deliberately forgettable dark clothing; ancestral swords worn as lineage markers, with formal dueling treated as a form of conversation rather than pure violence. **Secret:** Shogun Atsumori is very much alive — a survivor of four separate assassination attempts — and deliberately refuses to name an heir, believing a named successor is only a named target. Three rival daimyō circle that silence, and the recent resurfacing of the legendary Kurenai-ha (the Crimson Edge), a blade with an old customary right to name a claimant, has handed whoever holds it a lever to force the succession without ever drawing on the Shogun directly. Render Atsumori as watchful and armored even at rest, a man who has survived exactly as many blades as the rumor says, not a spectral stillness. **Symbols:** the katana as a near-sacred object; kami-marked natural features with small shrine markers; the Kurenai-ha itself, an excellent single-object hook for any succession-adjacent illustration.

#### Ayokha — the Monsoon Throne
**Touchstone:** an Angkor/Khmer-inspired mandala-kingdom radiating from a divine god-king who is, in practice, a puppet. **Palette:** gilded stone, jungle green, batik's wax-resist patterning, gold-and-silver thread. **Architecture:** massive step-pyramid temples (Mount Meru symbolism) covered in bas-relief; jungle-swallowed outer ruins; mangrove-delta port towns. **Dress/markers:** the Devaraja and royal guard in ornate ceremonial armor; batik textiles among commoners and nobles alike; betel-leaf offered as a hospitality ritual, worth including in any social/negotiation scene. **Economy of the sacred:** river-spirits ("nats") are bribed as a matter of course for safe passage, good harvests, and favorable rulings — river-shrines should look well-used and transactional (offerings of coin and food, not just flowers). **Secret:** the God-King is a puppet for his own court astrologer, a man who has not seen the sun in thirty years and controls the monsoon schedule — and with it the throne's legitimacy — through his readings. What he doesn't advertise: his predictions are no longer entirely his own. He keeps a silver coin that never spends, a cursed relic, floating in a jar of brine on his desk, and it whispers false promises of perfect rains into his readings. A striking, specific prop: an observatory desk dominated by a corked jar with something coin-bright glinting inside, never explained. **Symbols:** war elephants as a royal-power icon; temple bas-reliefs telling epic god-and-king narratives.

#### Aelaerem of the Eastern Realms — the Hollow's Neighbors
**Touchstone:** solitary, yamabushi-style hedge-keepers rather than the West's village hearth-communities. **Palette:** bamboo green, temple-stair stone grey, the same warding red-thread as the western Aelaerem but used sparingly, tied high on bamboo posts rather than at every doorway. **Material culture:** boundary management here is solitary and ascetic — a single hedge-keeper per stretch of border, rather than a community affair. **Design note:** keep the "Hollow" negotiation concept intact but underplay the cozy-hearth imagery entirely; this variant should read as austere and watchful rather than domestic.

#### Aelinnel of the Inland Sea — the Bell-Tenders
**Touchstone:** a tide-spirit negotiation culture built around copper bells rather than copper coinage. **Palette:** sea-green patina copper, tide-pool blue-grey, bell-bronze. **Numerology:** counting stops at eight here as well, echoing Theona and Aevrossa, but the taboo is enforced physically — ritual finger-loss is the penalty for a miscount during a bell-tending ceremony. This is a strong, specific body-modification detail: veteran bell-tenders should visibly be missing fingers, worn as a mark of long service rather than shame.

#### Lethai-al of Nihori — the Storm-Touched Wanderers
**Touchstone:** a landless, wandering shrine-circuit culture that travels a Nihori-analogue archipelago rather than settling. **Palette:** storm-grey, sea-salt white, faded shrine-vermillion. **Ritual imagery:** first-blood-only dueling with servitude (not death) as the stake; ancestor-name tide-charms worn as jewelry, each bead naming a forebear. **Secret:** the drowned kingdom that set this people wandering was sunk deliberately, by a grieving mother speaking a forbidden name — a mournful, quiet detail worth reflecting in this culture's shrine-circuit imagery (small floating shrines, never a fixed temple).

#### Lethai-ar of Ayokha — the River-Bound Oath-Keepers
**Touchstone:** river-witnessed contract culture serving the river deities Benzaiten and Inari. **Palette:** river-green, temple-gold, silk-white. **Material culture:** "witness cords" — silk cords that physically tighten on a liar mid-oath — function as this culture's lie-detector, paralleling Ecktoria's Everflame in a gentler, more intimate register. **Secret:** the God-King of Ayokha's own oath-cord is secretly broken — a subtle, damning detail for anyone who knows to look for it during a royal ceremony.

#### Lethai-thora of Sihai — the Silent Ledgers
**Touchstone:** a hidden-archive culture of high elves who fled here after "the Alder King withdrew and the Hazel Queen sealed her gates" — a direct tie to the Valewood's fall, treated as one continuous story told from two ends of the continent. **Palette:** ink-black archive shadow, muted parchment tones, a single cold accent color per archivist. **Cost of knowledge:** guarding forbidden knowledge here costs its keepers personal memories — a haunting parallel to the Vault-Queen's and Alder King's own losses; render elder archivists with a "quietly missing something" quality, intensified. **Secret:** the Sihai Emperor's own lineage is a forged secret, and it is these archivists, not the Censorate, who actually know the truth of it.

### 3.8 The Spaces Between

*This closing part covers the connective tissue of the setting — a border region, a canal city, a caravan camp, and three explicitly liminal, non-political "places" (the Ways Between, the Wilds, and Dungeons) that exist alongside rather than within the map. Treat these last three as tonal outliers: everywhere else in this document, ground the art in a specific culture's material reality; here, lean into dream-logic, weight, and patience instead.*

#### Ubral — the Stone Between Spears
**Touchstone:** Scottish highland clan code — cairns, hill-forts, oathstones — where the cairns themselves are the true government. **Palette:** heather purple-grey, weathered slate, peat brown, cold iron. **Architecture:** hill-forts (Dun Caerloch), pass-watch holdfasts with beacon towers (Keld Tor), dwarf-factor trading halls (Khaz Vurim's surface depots); cairns of stacked named stones growing taller every year, the oldest cairn nameless and remembered as "the first clan." **Dress/markers:** guest-tokens (a stone, bone, or braid of hair given by a last host) identify travelers at a glance — a cold token means long travel, no token at all marks a spy; clan badges in horn or stone, dwarf steel worn only when earned. **Ritual imagery:** wergild is paid in cattle driven into a cairn's shadow — if the herd grazes peacefully, the debt is forgiven; if it scatters, it isn't. **Secret:** the cairns are not symbolic — they are the actual government. Every named stone is a literal vote cast by a dead ancestor, and "cairn votes" (rolled for war, peace, or blood-price whenever an oath is broken) are the real seat of power in Ubral, outranking even the Lady of Tor. Render any council-of-cairns scene with the dead's verdict visibly split rather than unanimous — a lean of moss one way, a drift of mist the other — and reserve the oldest, nameless cairn, remembered only as "the first clan," as the founding fiction no one can name.

#### Silkstrand — City of Bridges & Dyewater
**Touchstone:** a Venetian canal-and-guild free port where water itself is treated as a keeper of memory and debt. **Palette:** dye-vat brights (indigo, madder red, saffron) breaking through an otherwise weathered brick-and-timber cityscape; canal water that shifts color with the season's dye run-off. **Architecture:** eight named bridges (Merchant's, Weaver's, Dyer's, Cooper's, Pilot's, Beggar's, Saint's) plus a ninth that only appears at low tide, leading to the drowned "Sunken Quarter" where debts are said to go to die. **Dress/markers:** a seal-ink stain on the right thumb marks an active merchant; fresh ink means newly in trade, faded ink means retired, no ink at all means "not a person" in the city's own cold reckoning. **Ritual imagery:** the annual Dyeing of the Waters festival turns the canals red, blue, green, and gold in sequence — an excellent, vivid set-piece image for the city, and a rare moment of pure visual joy to contrast with its harder edges. **Secret:** the city's true curse lives in the Archivolt's sealed archive of unwritten pacts — promises that were never formally stamped, which the Order of the Witness claims will bind anyone who reads them. The Red Clerk, who opened one anyway, now carries a seal that weeps blood instead of wax. The archive's ninth pact sits conspicuously blank, awaiting the Ragman's name — a strong image: ordinary bureaucratic clutter (stacked ledgers, wax seals) with one page deliberately, uncannily empty.

#### Midh-Akhaz — the Violet Gate
**Touchstone:** a lawless, walled-less border camp-town on the edge of the Violet Steppe — half caravan stop, half open wound. **Palette:** violet steppe-grass, felt-tent brown, dust-gold, the copper-and-blood tang of a place that runs on horse trade and quiet violence. **Architecture:** no walls, no gates — just rings of felt tents, latrine trenches, and a canvas-domed Coin-Weigh Tribunal that opens fresh every morning; a Dust Market that auctions goods (and sometimes questions) that "fell off a wagon, or off a corpse." **Dress/markers:** Ykrul warriors braid clan colors into their hair; Vilikari traders wear oiled braids strung with silver beads; loose braids mean a recent fight, cut braids mean a lost blood-price. **Ritual imagery:** the guest-cup escalation is the region's defining social ritual — a first pour is courtesy, a second is negotiation, a third an unspoken blood-price, and a fourth means the drinker is, socially speaking, already dead. **Secret:** the Oasis Compact's founding seal, believed lost, was actually stolen by a Vilikari factor still living under a false name in the Foreign Quarter — the seal itself is hidden inside a horse's skull buried beneath the camp's central Grass Ring. The skull belonged to the Khatun's own favorite stallion, and it did not die naturally.

#### The Ways Between — Spiritways & Veilways
*Use only for genuinely liminal/uncanny scenes, never for ordinary travel.* **Touchstone:** a dream-logic non-place where roads measure choices rather than distance, and every reflection is a version of the traveler who chose differently. **Palette:** directionless mist-grey and bruised twilight colors that don't map to any time of day; occasional intrusions of a single too-vivid color from "outside." **Visual approach:** avoid literal roads or landmarks; compose scenes around thresholds, bridges, and standing water (reflections) rather than terrain. Figures met here should be visually similar-but-wrong versions of people the viewer already knows, never wholly new characters. **Rule for illustrators:** never resolve where a path in the Ways Between actually leads — end every composition on an unanswered threshold, not a destination.

#### The Wilds — Roads, Ruins, and Weather
*Distinct in tone from the Ways Between: patient rather than dreamlike.* **Touchstone:** unclaimed borderland — old roads, ruins, and weather with more patience than any government. **Palette:** neutral, weather-worn greens and browns, with the region's mood color-coded by season. **Architecture:** cairns and trail-marks rather than buildings; forgotten fords with hermit shrines; ruins that predate any current nation, half-reclaimed by growth. **Ritual imagery:** travelers leave small offerings (a coin, a button, a lock of hair) at the site of any wrongdoing — the land's reaction (the offering taken, left, or moved) functions as a silent verdict. **Secret:** the Wilds have a literal heart, a warm, beating stone buried beneath the oldest oak — reserve this as a rare, high-mystery-tier reveal rather than a recurring image.

#### Dungeons — Living Infrastructure
*Reframes "dungeon crawl" spaces as living systems rather than static ruins.* **Touchstone:** ancient underground complexes that are not ruins but systems — built by Aeler, sealed by covenant, mined by Sidhi, or simply grown, like fungus, like a story told too many times. **Palette:** damp stone grey, fungus-bread pale green, cave-water black, the occasional warm lamp-glow of a room that "wants" to be found. **Design principle:** doors that open and close with no visible hand, corridors that rearrange when unwatched, and shadows that behave slightly wrong are more effective than overt monsters for signaling that a dungeon is alive. **Secret (rare, high-mystery-tier only):** the oldest dungeons are said to have a still-living architect sealed in their lowest chamber, kept alive by the structure's own will — if ever depicted, show them ancient and silent rather than menacing, a captive rather than a threat.

### 3.10 "If you only draw one thing" — hero shots by region

A single specific scene per region, for when there's only budget or time for one splash image, loading screen, or key-art piece. Each one packs in the palette, the material culture, and the region's "secret" hook in a single composable frame — no research required beyond this line.

- **Ecktoria:** A magistrate in a faded imperial-purple toga stands before the Everflame; a witness beside them sweats, throat tight, tongue already darkening at the edges. The fire, not the magistrate, is the real judge — light the scene so the flame is the brightest and most attentive-looking thing in it.
- **Acasia:** A toll-bridge at dusk. A lone figure crosses it holding a single lit lamp; their shadow on the stone behind them lags a half-step out of sync with their stride.
- **Vhasia:** A duel fought in a hall lined with faded hero-statues and museum placards. Two coats face off — one black-and-gold, one red-and-silver — neither side realizing the boy they're really fighting over is real, hidden away in the Queen-Mother's keep, and does not himself know if he's a prince or a puppet.
- **Thepyrgos:** A scholar on a sunlit stair-terrace stabilizes a flickering ward over a stack of ancient scrolls, sweat on their brow, a bound and faintly irritated spirit visibly holding up the archway behind them.
- **Viterra:** A dispute settled at a hedge-boundary — two seal rings held up side by side for comparison, oiled cloaks dripping fen-mist, no violence, just two rings and a held breath.
- **Ubral:** A herd of cattle driven into a cairn's long shadow at dusk, the drover watching to see whether they scatter or graze — the whole verdict of a feud riding on what the animals do next.
- **The Mistlands:** A farmstead at the ragged edge of a fog-bank. An eight-beat ward-chime hangs still on the lintel; a bowl of milk sits untouched on the step; the fog curls just short of the stone road, as if it knows better.
- **Ashaan:** A gilded, catacomb-adjacent room lit entirely by a single brass spirit-lantern — and the bound spirit inside is visibly watching the room's occupants rather than simply glowing for them.
- **Ykrul:** A winter camp at dusk; a Sky-Speaker holds a confident public pose before a ring of firelit faces, braids in clan-red catching the light — but their eyes, for one unguarded beat, don't believe a word of it.
- **Sihai:** A magistrate's writing table crowded with brass seals; held to the light, the stamped watermark on the document resembles a face no one will name, while behind the dais an unfinished imperial tomb's crack glows faintly, its whispered list of heirless dead emperors still growing.
- **Silkstrand:** A canal at low tide reveals a ninth bridge no one uses; dye-vat color still bleeds faintly into the water from the festival the week before.
- **The Valewood:** A traveler pauses at the edge of eight named, sunlit groves — and a ninth, unmarked path leading into a patch of forest with noticeably less color in it than the rest.

Use these as literal, ready-to-brief prompts; the fuller section 3 entries above exist for anyone who wants to build a second or third image for the same region.

## 4. Non-human peoples

Every non-human people below has its own coherent look — resist reusing "generic fantasy elf/dwarf/gnome" designs.

### Wood Elves (Lethai-al, "People of the Body")
Tall-to-lithe builds, copper-undertone skin (mahogany to olive; southern jungle populations skew darker with a faint green cast), keen low-light eyes. Dress in layered leathers, plant-dyed wool, bark-felt, bone/seed ornaments — minimal metal (a taboo, not a poverty). Northern bands read more taiga/vale-ranger; Crimson Basin and Crimson Valley cohorts read more canopy-monsoon with visible silk-craft and public "Mark" tattoos; Nihori's Lethai-al read as landless, storm-touched wanderers; Ayokha's Lethai-ar read as river-temple oath-keepers. Avoid elegant/ethereal high-fantasy tropes here — these are practical, ecological, ambush-doctrine (or, in the river/silk variants, contract-and-oath) cultures, not delicate archers in silver filigree.

### High Elves (Lethai-thora, "People of the Mind")
Fine-boned, luminous eyes, near-immobile poise, longevity in the 700–1000 year range. Layered fabrics with refractive weaves, muted palettes broken by exactly one precise accent color, memory-brooches. Largest open community lives in Thepyrgos; a hidden archivist community (the Silent Ledgers) lives in exile in Sihai, having fled the Valewood's fall. Important nuance for character design: they are quietly missing something — their own deep history was engineered away from them (see §9). Consider subtle visual tells of that loss: a slightly unfocused look when discussing their own past, ornamental "archive" tattoos or brooches that don't map to anything they can explain, or — in the Sihai archivists' case — a memory visibly costing them something each time forbidden knowledge is protected.

### "Dark Elves" (a foreign slur — call them Web-clans or Shed-cults)
Not a separate species: wood-elf lineages bound to two Sister-angels, originally from across the Great Sea. **Spider-bound:** pallid/ashen skin, veilwork and spider-ink facial filigree, web-silk lamellar. **Serpent-bound:** bronze/umber tones, scale-pattern tattoos, shed-scar rites, serpent-braided hair, fang/coil jewelry. Keep these two visually distinct from each other, not just from other elves.

### Gnomes (Aelinnel, "People of Sums")
Highland gnomes (Aevrossa): compact, stone-strong, slate-to-umber skin, hair in iron/rust/lichen-green, jeweler-callused hands, organized around an eights-numerology and a strict copper/iron/silver/gold status hierarchy. Forest gnomes: slighter, bark-and-leaf tones, moss-dyed cloaks. Inland Sea gnomes (the Bell-Tenders): tide-bell culture, veteran members visibly missing fingers from ritual miscount penalties. Faetae (winged): fine-boned, larger eyes, leathery/diaphanous wings sized to their body (gliders, not fliers). All obsess over precision tools, lapidary work, and math-script (knot-cords, abacus-songs, crystalline glyphs, coded copper messages) — good recurring prop design space.

### Halflings (Aelaerem, "People of the Hearth")
Small, sure-footed, chestnut-to-wheat hair, warm umber to rosy tan skin, leaf-dyed quilted jerkins, good hats, many pockets. **Design note specific to this people:** their homeland plays as cozy pastoral folk-horror — every warm, hearthy image should carry one quietly wrong detail (a scarecrow that's turned to watch, a stile counted "eight-and-one," a bee gone still, a red hearth-knot left untied). The Eastern Realms variant strips away the cozy communal imagery in favor of solitary, ascetic hedge-keepers — same underlying "negotiation with the Hollow" concept, much starker presentation. This duality (warmth with a hidden negotiation running under it) is the entire visual point of the Aelaerem; don't illustrate either variant as purely twee or purely grim.

### Dwarves (Aeler, "People of Stone")
Functional grandeur is the aesthetic core: polished basalt, inlaid metal, runic ledgers set into lintels — nothing purely decorative. Mountain-hall dwarves (the Vault-Queen's Aeler) vs. hillhold dwarves vs. urban trade-enclave "Stone Court" dwarves vs. the high-altitude Himadri Foothills miners all get slightly different built environments (deep citadel / terrace-mine wind-fort / walled embassy-counting-house / breath-counted mine shaft), but shared guild heraldry (Mason, Smith, Foundry, Counter, Brewer, Teamster, Lampers) unifies them visually across borders. Bell- and breath-counting as literal safety infrastructure is a strong, specific Aeler visual signature wherever they appear.

### Other Peoples ("Beyond the Old Roads")
Faekin (fairies, centaurs, fauns, forest gnomes), beastfolk (catfolk, avians, dragonfolk, snakefolk, seafolk, turtlefolk), giantkin (Stonemen/Goliaths, Greenmen/Firbolgs), and — far to the west, rarely seen — goblinkind empires. These appear at borders, fey fairs, and beacon-post networks rather than as settled "nations" the way humans/elves/dwarves are. Render contact points (border shrines with bell-ropes, colored pennons marking market-days, river fords with centaur heralds) rather than trying to build full material cultures for each.

## 7. Magic: visual language

### The Eight Elements (color/motif wheel)
Use this table as the base palette for any spell effect, ward, or magical VFX — consistent element-to-color mapping keeps unrelated illustrations legible as "the same magic system."

| Element | Domain | Manifestation | Backlash visual |
|---|---|---|---|
| Earth | stone, soil, metal, structure | shaping/sensing/moving earth | rigid cracking, collapse, things in stone "waking up" |
| Fire | flame, heat, light, change | ignition, heating, purification | uncontrolled spread, scorching |
| Wind | air, breath, sound, flight | movement, drafts, amplified sound | whipping chaos, dispersal |
| Water | liquids, tides, rain, flow | calming, directing, purifying | stagnation, flooding, contamination |
| Fate | time, prophecy, causality | seeing threads, nudging destiny | paradox imagery, temporal loop visuals |
| Life | health, growth, birth, death | healing, growth, vitality | overgrowth, decay, drained vitality |
| Fortune/Luck | randomness, chance | tilting probability | ironic reversals, "bad luck" sight gags |
| Death/Dreams | sleep, death, spirits, the void | speaking with the dead, dream work | hauntings, nightmares, unwanted spiritual attention |

**Rule of thumb for illustrating any spellcaster:** decide which 1–2 Elements they draw on, pick the matching color/motif above, and always leave room in the composition for the Backlash — a caster mid-working should look like they're negotiating, not commanding.

### Arts of magic
Common Arts (Alchemancy, Herbomancy, Geomancy, Hydromancy, Pyromancy, Illusiomancy) should look like craft — workshops, reagents, tools — even when the effect is dramatic. Forbidden Arts (Thanatomancy, Voidmancy, Domimancy) should look transgressive even at rest: unnatural stillness, wrongness in reflections, spaces that don't hold light correctly. Never illustrate a Forbidden Art caster as merely "edgy-cool" — the world's aesthetic reserves genuine dread for these three.

### The Three Forged (major relic blades)
Three fallen-angel blades recur in high myth-tier content — useful for legendary weapon and artifact design:
- **Remorse** ("the Lamented Edge"): a blade that cuts to make the wielder *remember*, not to wound. Visual language: dull, sorrowful shine; edges that seem to bend light backward; carried by someone marked by guilt, not glory.
- **Mantle** ("the Gilded Breath"): a crown-making blade currently swallowed and silenced inside a dragon's hoard. Visual language: gold and scale, muffled brightness, dreaming stillness — if drawn at all, draw it dormant, half-buried in coin.
- **Hegemon** ("the Word That Walks"): not a physical blade at all but a spoken contagion — persuasion given will. Has no consistent physical form; if personified, render as breath, ink, or a crowd's shared expression rather than an object.

## 8. Creatures — regional bestiary style notes

Group creature design by the region it's tied to rather than by generic "monster type":

- **Acasia (cursed marches):** Blackwood Wolves (unnaturally intelligent, bone-shrine markers), Curse Echoes (flickering trauma re-enactments), Thorn Beasts (living wood/thorn, amber eyes), Bone-Singers (skeletal, skin-robes, bone instruments).
- **Mistlands (bells and breath):** Mist Wraiths, Bell-Spirits, Salt-Wights, Pall Guardians, Fog Crawlers, Marsh Hags, Drowned Knights.
- **The Valewood (empire under leaves):** Redcaps (blood-soaked caps, oath-breakers' tormentors), Echo-Legionaries (translucent soldiers replaying old battles), Fox-Headed Couriers (fae messengers, glowing eyes), Ancient Tree-Spirits, Web Weavers, Bark Golems, Seasonal Sprites.
- **Aeler (crowns and under-vaults):** Stone Giant Elders (granite skin, inner fire eyes), Deep Drakes (black-glass scales, light-absorbing eyes), Vault Wardens, Geomantic Constructs, Crystal Sentinels.
- **Ykrul (wolf standards):** Dire Wolves, Steppe Wargs, Kurgan Wights, Sky-Spirit manifestations, Wind Riders, Horse Spirits.
- **Dungeons (living infrastructure):** treat inhabitants less as a fixed bestiary and more as symptoms of the space itself — animated architecture, rearranging corridors, and a captive "architect" rather than roaming monsters.

**A note on "named" monsters** (the Weeping Knight, the Merchant of Curiosities, the Hollow Crown, the Iron Shepherd, etc.): these are one-of-a-kind tragic or ambiguous figures, not monster-manual archetypes to reuse. Illustrate them with a specific, sympathetic backstory detail visible in the design (the Weeping Knight's tarnished silver armor and pale-glowing greatsword; the Iron Shepherd's mismatched salvaged-armor plating and its flock of scrap-metal mechanical sheep) rather than generic "spooky."

## 9. The cosmic substrate (use sparingly, at high mystery-tier only)

Beneath the political-fantasy surface, the deep lore reveals a much older wound: refugee humans fleeing a reality-consuming force arrived in this world and nearly destroyed it; the native moon/high elves won an interdimensional war against them at ruinous cost, then had their own history deliberately erased by three cosmic sisters to prevent the catastrophe from recurring. This should almost never be illustrated directly — it's a background truth the setting's inhabitants don't know. Where it should surface visually:

- **Ancient ruins that feel like they're from three different architectural traditions layered on top of each other** — this is the single most useful visual tell for "something from the old war" versus an ordinary ruin.
- **Aeler (dwarven) infrastructure carrying an unplaceable heaviness** — technically sound, beautifully built, but scenes set in the oldest dwarven halls (or the Himadri Foothills' sealed mine) can carry a faint "this place has seen something" wrongness (in lighting, in the way sound behaves, never in anything overtly supernatural).
- **High elf unease around old books and artifacts** — a high-elf character recoiling from, or refusing to touch, an ancient object is a meaningful visual beat, not a quirk; the Sihai archivists pay for this knowledge in memory, a direct, personal cost worth depicting.
- **The Ninth / the Beyond** — a continent-spanning motif: the ninth cup, knot, bell, breath, wave, wind, beat, coal, and lantern-signal recur across nearly every culture in section 3 as the place where "the Hollow" gets in. Use groupings of eight as a quiet, setting-wide visual habit, and reserve a marked or avoided ninth as a dread-cue an illustrator can drop into almost any region's otherwise ordinary scene.
- **Fraudulent, absent, dead, or puppeted rulers** — nearly every region in section 3 hides exactly this kind of secret at its top. This rhymes thematically with the cosmic substrate (nothing at the top of this world is what it claims to be) and can be treated as the setting's *political* mirror of its *cosmic* one.

Treat this section as a rare seasoning, not a base ingredient — most Fate's Edge art should read as grounded political/regional fantasy first.

## 11. Visual off-limits — a "do not draw" list

A short, direct list for anyone briefing an artist or an image-generation prompt who doesn't have time to read the rest of this document. If nothing else survives, these should:

- **Do not draw pristine, shiny plate armor.** Everything is patched, mismatched, or scavenged. If armor looks factory-new, the viewer should read that as a warning sign about the wearer, not admire it.
- **Do not draw clean, glowing magic with no visible cost.** Every working shows its price — clinging smoke, a trembling hand, ice that melts wrong, a wrong-tempered flame color. Reserve genuinely "clean" magic for the rare, explicitly graceful exceptions (dawnfire), and undercut even those slightly.
- **Do not draw the Ninth explicitly.** Never illustrate a literal ninth cup, knot, bell, wave, or figure as the visible payoff of a scene — imply it through a marked or avoided ninth object, or through a group deliberately composed in eights.
- **Do not unmask the Maeri sisters of Ashaan.** All three (Veiled, Helmed, Masked) are always exactly seven feet tall with a fully hidden face — no exceptions.
- **Do not draw a "chosen one" pose or a perfect kingdom.** No haloed heroes, no flawless courts. Every ruler in this setting is a fraud, a puppet, a prisoner, dead-and-impersonated, or worse — compose authority figures with that undertow even when the secret itself is off-page.
- **Do not use generic "epic" wide-angle battle shots.** Favor desolate wide landscapes for scale and isolation, and tight, intimate framing for cost and consequence — never a triumphant mass-battle establishing shot.
- **Do not give the Forbidden Arts (Thanatomancy, Voidmancy, Domimancy) an "edgy-cool" treatment.** They should read as genuinely transgressive and unsettling even at rest, not stylish.
- **Do not depict the Ways Between, the Wilds' living heart, or Pereshi's "ninth coal" casually or often.** These are rare, high-mystery-tier reveals — using them as recurring or background imagery drains their weight.

## 13. Horror Campaigns expansion

*A tonal and visual appendix for the Horror Campaigns line — a separate register from the rest of this document. Where sections 1–12 describe grounded political-fantasy regions with a hidden rot underneath, this appendix covers material that is meant to be felt as horror from the first frame, not slowly revealed as one. Draw from this section only when a piece is explicitly Horror Campaigns content — mixing this register into an ordinary regional illustration will read as tonally off.*

### 13.1 The general horror vibe

**Genre touchstones:** cosmic horror (the unknown is worse than the known; comprehension itself is dangerous) layered with psychological/body horror (debt, addiction, and grief made literal and physical) and a found-document conceit — much of this material is framed in-world as a compiled, annotated archive rather than an objective narrative, so illustrations can lean into the idea of being "evidence" (marginalia, water damage, a hand that drew this while afraid) rather than clean art.

**Palette:** blood-red and aged parchment as the two anchor tones — reserve pure blood-red for title treatments and the most severe reveals, and let everything else sit in desaturated, candlelit ambers and grays. Black should read as absence, not shadow: a Horror Campaigns black should feel like it is *removing* detail from the world rather than simply underlighting it.

**Composition rule specific to this material:** favor tight, intimate framing even more aggressively here than elsewhere — a trembling hand, a held breath, an object described in obsessive close-up (a coin, a thread, a tooth) does more work than a monster shown in full. When something must be shown large, keep part of it unresolved or geometrically wrong rather than fully legible.

**A useful throughline:** nearly every faction and creature in this appendix runs on the idea that horror has *paperwork* — debts are recorded, prices are named, bargains are kept to the letter. Visually, this means ledgers, threads, tattoos, coins, and contracts recur constantly as the horror's actual instruments, more than claws or fangs do. When in doubt, illustrate the bureaucracy of the horror, not just its teeth.

### 13.2 Malachai, the Chained Angel

**Touchstone:** a bound, patient, sorrowful captor-goddess — less a monster to defeat than a debt to eventually pay. **Core physical description (keep exact across all depictions):** wings bound to stone, mouth sewn shut with silver wire, eyes open and patient rather than angry, voice issuing from a wound in her throat rather than her mouth ("the rasp of rust and honey"). She hangs in chains of a black iron that visibly *drinks* light rather than reflecting it; render any scene with her chains as having a small dead zone of extra-dark shadow around the metal itself. **Setting:** a sunken ziggurat city, desert-buried, its deepest chapel lit only by whatever light source a visitor brings.

**Iconography (her "signs," useful as recurring small props across any Malachai-touched character or shrine):** a silver coin that cannot be spent except on suffering; a thorn that draws blood but never heals; a mouth that smiles out of sync with the rest of a face; a bell that rings exactly once and never again. **Regional name variants** — treat these as the same iconography reskinned with local material culture, not new designs.

**The First Vampires:** a shattered holy order who drank her blood in desperation and became her eternal, sunlight-immune servants — render them in worn, once-fine white silk bearing a half-remembered order sigil, aristocratic and tragic rather than menacing; their leader is explicitly a reluctant, bored monster who turns victims because killing no longer interests him, not out of cruelty. **The Worn** — those who fully succumb to her curse — are hollowed ghoul-servants with peeling skin, cracked-glass eyes, and limbs that move at the wrong speed (four visual subtypes: Shambler, Screamer, Stalker, Crackler — vary their wrongness by *tempo* of movement rather than by silhouette). **The Well of Ichor**, deep beneath the ziggurat, is a black, honey-and-copper-scented pool that cures any Malachai curse at the cost of a permanent memory — a rare, tempting, genuinely dangerous-looking image, guarded by silent hollow-eyed children called the Silent Ones.

### 13.3 Daughters of the Unbroken Cord

**Touchstone:** an invisible, centuries-deep mutual-aid infrastructure — midwives, hospice-keepers, alms-house matrons — that turns birth, illness, and death into ledgered debt. **Motto (use directly in any signage, shrine, or promotional in-world material):** "The soup is always free. The price never is." **Palette:** undyed wool and ash-grey, warmed only by candlelight and the amber of tea or broth — this faction should look like comfort right up until the viewer notices the thread.

**Signature iconography — the gray thread:** a thin, non-physical gray cord that wraps around the throat or wrist of anyone in the Daughters' debt, pulsing faintly, visible only to those who know to look for it. This is the single most useful recurring image for the whole faction — a Daughter-touched character's design should include this thread as a quiet, easy-to-miss detail rather than an obvious tattoo or brand. **The Flesh-Codex:** a Daughter's own rites are tattooed directly onto her body rather than written in any book — render these tattoos as subtly alive (a faint weep, an occasional line that seems to have moved since last seen).

**The Hollowed:** the faction's silent ritual witnesses — children (or animals) whose hearts have been replaced with a bundle of gray thread and the ash of a forgotten name, their eyes sewn open with silver wire. They never eat, sleep, age, or speak. This is a genuinely disturbing image and should be handled with restraint: a Hollowed figure standing motionless at the edge of a warm, domestic scene (a birth, a deathbed, a hearth) is far more effective than a close-up of the sewn eyes themselves. **Hierarchy (three offices, useful for NPC design):** the Keeper of the Door, the Keeper of the Name (who loses a finger for every debt she forgets — render her hands accordingly), and the Keeper of the Price (who has lost all sense of heat and burns unnoticed — can be shown standing unbothered near an open flame or a wound she hasn't registered). **The High Mother** herself should never be depicted directly — she dwells in a non-place, and per this document's general restraint principle, is best represented by her absence: an empty chair at the head of a table, a door that shouldn't be there.

**Crossover note:** this faction and Malachai's are explicit rivals with near-identical hollow-eyed servant types — when both appear together, differentiate them by *texture* rather than concept: gray thread versus silver wire and light-drinking black iron, so a viewer can tell at a glance which debt a given victim actually owes.

### 13.4 The Whispers adventures

**General vibe:** claustrophobic, escalating, and built on the idea that knowledge and proximity are themselves the danger — the deeper or longer a character investigates, the more the world's geometry and their own allies start to betray them. This material is about **comprehension made dangerous** — render investigation scenes (reading, listening, descending) with the same dread as a physical threat.

**Whispers in the Tunnels** (beneath Thepyrgos): flooded, sealed maintenance tunnels where an ancient psychic entity has no physical form at all — it exists as "a pattern of thought that seeps through stone and water." Illustrate its presence through environmental wrongness (water that ripples with no current, stone that seems to listen) rather than as a creature design. Victims corrupted by contact become corrupted in three escalating visual stages — start subtle (slate-grey skin, milky eyes) and let later stages become genuinely reality-breaking (a "walking tear in reality" that speaks in the voices of people the viewer loves) — never skip straight to the extreme stage, the escalation is the horror. Setting notes worth using directly: a Bone Market where ghost-merchants trade in memories rather than coin, and a submerged throne room implied to exist in more than one dimension at once (render with impossible, slightly-wrong perspective rather than obvious sci-fi distortion).

**Whispers in the Stacks** (a university archive): a library where corridors shift and rooms are larger within than without, and staircases spiral into themselves, leading not upward but inward — turning the setting's usual sun-bleached scholar-city warmth into something oppressive. Keep the palette recognizably warm (marble, olive, sea-blue) but let light behave wrong: shadows that fall from the wrong angle, sconces that don't quite reach as far as they should. A key NPC is described with eyes that shift color and seem to "contain entire galaxies," and a voice that "echoes from vast distances" — a strong design instruction is to keep his surface presentation calm and scholarly, letting only these two details (eyes, voice) hint at what's underneath.

### 13.5 Horror Campaigns — quick visual recipe & hero shots

| Subject | Quick recipe | "If you only draw one thing" |
|---|---|---|
| Malachai | Bound wings + sewn silver-wire mouth + light-drinking black chains | Her patient, open eyes catching the only light in an otherwise pitch-dark sunken chapel, chains disappearing into darkness above and below her |
| The Daughters of the Unbroken Cord | Undyed wool + ash grey + a near-invisible gray thread at a throat | A midwife pours tea by candlelight for a grieving father while, unnoticed at the edge of the room, a Hollowed child stands motionless with silver-wire eyes |
| Whispers in the Tunnels | Black floodwater + slate-grey corrupted skin + rippling stone | A flooded tunnel junction where the water ripples in slow, deliberate patterns with no current to explain them |
| Whispers in the Stacks | Marble/olive/sea-blue + wrong-angle shadow + spiral stairs turning inward | A university stair spiraling inward toward a point that shouldn't exist, lit by sconces whose light doesn't reach quite as far as it should |

**Additional off-limits notes for this appendix:** do not depict the High Mother directly — represent her only through absence. Do not give the deep tunnel entity a physical body — it is a pattern, not a creature. Do not over-use the Hollowed's sewn eyes in close-up; their stillness at a scene's edge is the scarier image. Reserve blood-red as a title/accent color, not a general horror palette — overusing it flattens the dread this material is built on.
